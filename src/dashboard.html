Scanning

Sends a scan request to every scannable device, so its scan response arrives too — that is often where the device name is. Off = passive: the scanner never transmits and is invisible to the devices around it, but sees fewer names.
Picks up BLE 5 long-range advertisers, which few devices use. The radio then splits its time between the 1M and Coded PHYs, so it catches fewer packets from ordinary devices.

Logging

For field use: the board begins a session (named “auto”) as soon as it boots with an SD card in.
Wi-Fi and Bluetooth share one radio. While Wi-Fi is on — even just the access point — the scanner only gets part of the air time and misses some packets. With this on, Wi-Fi switches off 5 s after logging starts, so the scanner listens 100 % of the time; this dashboard is unreachable until logging stops. Stop with a POWER double-click — Wi-Fi then comes back by itself.
A single click while logging places a marker row in the log; double-click starts/stops logging; a 3 s hold powers off.
For live capture on a PC (rows start with BTS,); works with or without an SD session. A slow serial reader can make the scanner drop reports.

Target devices

One rule per line. A device matching any rule is flagged as a target: a TARGET row is logged, the LED flashes magenta, and it's listed on the Dashboard.

Scanning is continuous with no duplicate filter: every advertisement and scan response is received, legacy and BLE 5 extended. Wi-Fi shares the same radio, so while Wi-Fi is on the scanner gets less than 100 % of the air time. Reports the radio controller itself discards under heavy load are not counted here.

Motion sensor

±4 g (default) covers walking, running and handling; use ±8/16 g only if hard knocks clip. The IIS3DWB samples at 26.7 kHz; the board averages that down to ~100 Hz. Its filter stays off — a high-pass would remove gravity, which tilt and step detection need.
Each logging session then also gets <name>.motion.csv (time, x, y, z in g, ~3 KB/s) on the same clock as the scan log, for your own analysis. The once-per-second MOTION rows in the scan log are written either way.

What the board can tell from acceleration alone (no gyroscope, no compass): still / moving / walking, steps and cadence, an estimated walking speed and distance, the main horizontal axis it moves along (relative to the board, 180° ambiguous, needs a few seconds to settle after the board is turned) and its tilt. No absolute heading and no position tracking.

Normally the device joins the strongest AP of its network. Pin it to one specific AP so several BTScanner devices share the same access point — needed for time-aligned (TSF) recordings. If that AP is out of range it falls back to a normal connect, so this never strands the device.
